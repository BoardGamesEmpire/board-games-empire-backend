# household

Households: trust groups of any size whose members share their games and plan play together. This library serves the household, roster, invite and quota routes under `/api/households`.

The server decides what each request may do, and this table is what it grants. A client can read it, by the viewer's household role and the household's member invite and member share policies, to hide what the viewer can't do. The server still refuses anything the table says No to.

`src/lib/household-role-table.spec.ts` asks each role's real permissions every question in this table, and compares the table here with its own. A change to either fails until both agree.

- **Granted by User:** these rows come from User, the base role every registered user holds, not from the household role. No household role can take them away. Narrowing one for guests would mean changing the User role's own permission.
- **Only a household owner acts on the household admin role (#168).** A household admin changes the household role of, removes, invites and revokes the invites of people with the member or guest role. Nobody changes their own household role. A household admin who wants to step down asks an owner, or leaves.
- **The member invite policy (#166)** decides how people with the member role invite: directly, for a household owner's or admin's approval, or not at all. Household owners and admins always invite directly, and household guests never invite.
- **The member share policy (#810)** decides how people with the member role share a game they created with the household: directly, for a household owner's or admin's approval, or not at all. Household owners and admins always share directly, and household guests never share with the household. A share waiting for approval grants the household nothing until it is approved.
- **Your own game shares need no household role (#810).** Whoever shared a game removes their own share, and whoever created a game sees its shares, whatever their household role: these come from User. So the table's rows on seeing, approving and removing the household's game shares are about other members' shares, and its three rows on sharing a game are about one the viewer created.
- **A household's game shares are listed and approved on the household (#810).** `GET /api/households/:householdId/game-shares` lists every share into the household, waiting ones included, each with the game's id, title and thumbnail, so an approver can tell what they are approving. `POST /api/households/:householdId/game-shares/:shareId/approve` puts a waiting share in effect. Approving a share asks whether the person who made it could still share it, directly or for approval, with their household role and the member share policy as they are at that moment, as approving an invite asks of its sender. If the sharer has since been demoted to guest or banned, or the policy switched to Off, the approval is refused and the share stays waiting until someone removes it. After a switch to Direct it stays approvable. Turning a share down is removing it, at `DELETE /api/games/:id/shares/:shareId`.
- **Withdrawing an invite you sent** needs no permission (#164). Revoking is cancelling an invite someone else sent.
- **Leaving a household, or being removed from it, revokes invites (#747).** The invites the person sent and still open are revoked, and so are open invites to them by id. Invites to their email address stay open, because revoking them would show each sender whose address it was. So someone removed while an invite to their verified address is open can still accept it and rejoin, until it expires or a household owner or admin revokes it. A household ban closes that gap (#782).
- **Leaving a household, or being removed from it, ends the person's game shares into it (#810).** Every share into the household of a game they created ends, waiting ones included, as the invites they sent are revoked (#747): the standing to share into the household was the membership's. Their shares into other households stay.
- **Banning someone (#782).** A household owner or admin can ban someone with the member or guest role, or someone who isn't a member. Only a household owner can ban a household admin or another owner. Banning a member removes them from the household, which ends their game shares into it as leaving does (#810). Whether they were a member or not, the invites that leaving or being removed revokes are revoked: the ones they sent, and the open invites to them by id. The open invites to their email address are withheld instead: they still look sent to whoever sent them, and nobody can accept them. Nobody is notified of the ban, the banned user included. While the ban stands, an invite to them by id is refused, an invite to their address is stored withheld, and no admission lets them in: a banned user who reaches one is told plainly that they can't become a member. Lifting the ban opens every way back in: they can be invited by id again, an admission lets them in, and the open invites to their address that were withheld reach them, as if just sent, and they're told of each. An invite stays withheld only if it would be withheld when sent today, because the person blocked its sender, or their preferences don't let its sender invite them. An invite the ban revoked stays revoked; a new one can be sent.
- **Accepting an invite checks that its sender could still send it (#747).** The check uses the sender's household role and the member invite policy as they are at that moment. If the sender has left, been demoted or banned, or the policy has been tightened since, the invite is revoked and the person accepting gets the same error as for any revoked invite. Someone who is already a member gets the already-a-member error instead, and nothing is revoked. An invite that an owner or admin approved still stands if the sender could send it for approval.
- **Approving an invite asks the same question.** A household owner or admin can't approve an invite whose sender could no longer send it, directly or for approval: the approval is refused, takes nothing from the day's invite allowance, and the invite stays waiting for approval until someone revokes it.
- **Invites don't come with the household.** Reading the household (`GET /api/households/:id`) reaches everyone who can see it, a member's friends included, so it carries no invites (#665). Listing the household's invites (`GET /api/households/:householdId/invites`), past ones included, is a household owner's or admin's, and their list adds each invitee's address (#167). An inviter finds the invites they sent at `GET /api/invites/sent`.
- **The last owner** can't leave or be removed until they transfer ownership.
- **Event roles** (host, co-host and the rest) are separate from household roles, and someone who creates a household event hosts it. Household owners and admins edit, cancel and reopen the household's events, and cancel their occurrences, but archiving an event is its host's and co-host's (#795), deleting an event is its host's (#567), and deleting an occurrence belongs to the event's own roles.
- **Restoring a deleted household** is open to any of its owners during the restore window. The deletion grants it, one household at a time, rather than a household role (#175).
- **Plays** have no routes yet (#478), so their rows say what the household roles will hold once they do.
- **Every household role is one of these four.** A household never writes roles of its own. It will add permissions through a variant of a shipped household role (#844), and take them away per household (#845); either way the member keeps one of these four. Until either ships, a client may read this table by role name. After that the role name no longer says everything a member may do, and the API owes the client a field saying what the viewer may do (#844, #845). `docs/adr/0001-households-never-write-roles.md` records why (#169).

## The game pool and excluded games (#367, #170)

A household's game pool is the games its members share with it. A collection entry is in the game pool when its owner has collection sharing on in that household and hasn't made it an excluded game there. Collection sharing is on by default, and an owner's own entries count like anyone's.

- **`GET /api/households/:id/games`** lists the game pool a page at a time, ordered by title, then id. Each row is a game with its `owners`, one per collection entry that puts it in the game pool, so a member who owns it on two platforms is named twice. Owners are ordered by shown name, then member, then entry id, so one member's entries stay together. `total` counts games, not entries. Pages are offsets into a live list, so a change between requests can shift the rows after it.
- **What the caller sees is capped by their own reads.** An entry shows only if the GameCollection read ceiling lets the caller read it, and a game only if the Game read ceiling lets them open it. An entry or game they can't read is left out and not counted. The ceiling is an approximation: an entry excluded from one household the caller shares with its owner is left out of another household's game pool too, so the game pool never serves more than the collection read would.
- **`gamePoolReach`** (`Private` or `Friends`) is who may open the game pool besides members and guests. It is a door, not a grant: a friend let in sees only the entries they could already read. It is never wider than the household's own reach. Setting `Friends` while the household is `Private` is refused, and narrowing the household to `Private` lowers `gamePoolReach` to `Private` in the same write. Anyone else, server staff included, is refused as `GET /api/households/:id` refuses them: 403 when the household exists, 404 when it doesn't.
- **`PATCH /api/households/:householdId/members/me`** turns the caller's collection sharing on or off. Their excluded games stay out either way.
- **Excluded games are the caller's own entries only.** `GET` and `POST /api/households/:householdId/members/me/excluded-games` list and add them, and `DELETE …/excluded-games/:collectionEntryId` puts one back. These routes act on the caller's own membership whatever their household role. An excluded game of an entry the caller removed from their collection is not listed and answers 404 to `DELETE`; it applies again if the entry comes back. Adding one again keeps when it was first excluded; a reason sent replaces the stored one, `null` or a blank one clears it, and leaving it out keeps it, so a retry never wipes the note. The reason is the member's own note: it is returned only to them, and never appears in an audit entry. Excluding a game or putting one back sends no webhook, since a webhook on the household reaches the readers the roster keeps excluded games from (#868). Turning collection sharing on or off does send one, since every roster read shows it.

## How a household role is set

Each change to someone's household role has one path, and no other server code sets one (#169). The e2e fixtures write role rows directly, to arrange a household before any request is sent. Removing a member, leaving, or being banned deletes the role along with the membership. A new path, such as guardianship, is added here when it is designed.

| Change                                                                                              | The only path                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                        |
| --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| A founder becomes household owner                                                                   | Creating the household (`HouseholdService.create`)                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                   |
| A newcomer gets the household admin, member or guest role                                           | Accepting an invite. The role the invite offers is checked when it's sent (`CreateHouseholdInviteDto`) and when it's accepted (`HouseholdInviteResponder`), and written by `HouseholdMemberService.addMemberWithin`, which refuses the household owner role and returns an existing member unchanged |
| An existing member moves between the household admin, member and guest roles                        | `PATCH /api/households/:householdId/members/:memberId/role` (`HouseholdMemberService.updateMemberRole`)                                                                                                                                                                                              |
| An existing member becomes household owner, and the owner who hands it over becomes household admin | Transferring ownership (`POST …/members/:memberId/transfer-ownership`), and nothing else (#158)                                                                                                                                                                                                      |
| A member is banned, and their household role goes with their membership                             | Banning them (`POST /api/households/:householdId/bans`, `HouseholdBanService.ban`), which removes them through `HouseholdMemberService.removeUserWithin`, asked as removing them is asked (#782)                                                                                                     |
| A join request is approved (#231, not built yet)                                                    | Will go through `addMemberWithin`                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                    |

The invite and the role change share one rule for which roles can be given: `ASSIGNABLE_HOUSEHOLD_ROLES` and `isAssignableHouseholdRole`, beside `UpdateMemberRoleDto`. Every path but creating the household looks the role up through `resolveHouseholdRoleId`; creating the household connects the household owner role by name.
