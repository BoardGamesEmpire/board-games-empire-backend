# Privacy policy

This policy explains what this server keeps about you, why it keeps it, and where it goes.

## Who keeps your data

This server is run by its operator: the person or group who set it up and looks after it. Your data is kept on this server, under the operator's control.

Board Games Empire is software that anyone can run on a server of their own. The people who make it don't run this server. Nothing you store here is sent to them.

## What the server stores

- **Your account:** your email address and username, and your first and last name if you give them.
- **How you sign in:** your sign-in details, such as your password, passkeys and two-factor settings, and any sign-in providers you have linked.
- **Your sessions:** when you signed in, the browser or app you used, and the network address you connected from.
- **Your profile and settings:** your display name and picture. What you add to your profile, such as an about-me text, your pronouns, where you are, how you like to play, and any dietary or accessibility needs. Your settings, such as whether people can send you friend requests, and the plugins you have switched on.
- **Your games:** your collection, with your ratings, comments and favorites; for each copy of a game, when and where you got it, what you paid, what it is worth, its condition and edition, your notes about it, and when and why it left your collection; and how your ratings changed over time. Your lists and your wishlist, and the games you share with other people. Games you remove from your collection stay listed as previously owned. Games you import or add to the server's game catalog are recorded as added by you.
- **Accounts on other services:** if you link your Steam account, the server keeps your SteamID and the address of your Steam profile, and if you link your BoardGameGeek account, your BoardGameGeek username and the address of your profile there. Unlinking deletes them.
- **Households, events and friends:** the households you belong to and your role in each. The events you create or are invited to, with your RSVPs, availability, the games you offer, nominations and votes. The invites you send and receive. Your friendships, friend requests and blocks.
- **People without an account:** when someone invites a person to a household by email address, or adds someone without an account to an event, the server keeps the name and email address they give.
- **Bans:** if you are banned from the server, any reason given and when the ban ends. If a household bans you, the ban and any reason given.
- **Media:** the files you upload, such as pictures and documents, with their names and descriptions. Media you contribute to the server becomes public, and stays credited to you.
- **Notifications:** the messages the server shows you in the app, such as invites, RSVPs and finished imports.
- **API keys and webhooks:** the API keys you create for other programs, and the webhooks you set up, with the web address each one calls.
- **Feedback reports:** the bug reports, crash reports and feature requests you send from the app, with the details about the app and your device that come with them.
- **The audit log:** a record of the changes made on the server: what changed, who made the change, and when.

## Why it is kept

The server keeps your data to do what you use it for:

- to sign you in and keep your account safe;
- to keep your games and lists, and show them to the people you choose;
- to plan events and run households with the people in them;
- to tell you about things that concern you;
- to let the people who run the server keep it in order and fix problems.

## Where it stays

Your data is kept in the database and file storage the operator runs for this server.

The server also writes logs and monitoring data about the requests it answers. The logs include the network address each request came from. The operator decides where logs and monitoring data are kept and for how long, and may send them to a monitoring service of their choosing.

## What leaves the server

Some features send data to other services. This happens only in these cases:

- **You search for a game or import one.** The server sends your search words, or the game to import, and the language to use, to the DataGateways the server admins added. A DataGateway looks up games at a game-data service, which may be on the internet.
- **The server sends you email,** such as a link to reset your password. The email goes through the mail service set in the server's settings, with your email address.
- **You set up a webhook.** The server sends news of changes to the web address you gave, only about things you can still see, and only if the server allows that address.
- **You sign in through a sign-in provider.** The provider the operator set up learns that you signed in to this server. It sends the server your name, email address and picture.
- **You link your Steam account.** The server sends you to Steam to sign in. Steam tells the server your SteamID, and the server asks Steam to confirm the answer is genuine.
- **You link your BoardGameGeek account.** The server asks BoardGameGeek, through the DataGateway the server admins added, whether the username exists.
- **A plugin sends data out.** Plugins the server admins install can send data to other services. They do so only with the permissions consented to them, and only to addresses the server allows.
- **The operator sends out logs and monitoring data.** The operator can have the server send its logs and monitoring data to a monitoring service they choose.

## Who can see it

- **You** can see what you store.
- **Other people** see what you choose to show them. Your profile, collection, lists, media and events, and the games you create, each have a reach: how far beyond you they can be seen. A reach can be only you, your friends, people further out in your network of friends, or everyone on the server. Everyone means everyone with an account on this server. For your collection and the games you create, it also means anyone who can reach the server, even without an account. Your households have a reach too, but it never goes as far as everyone. The details of your copies (what you paid for one, what it is worth, where and when you got it, and your notes about it), including copies you no longer have, and how your ratings changed over time are only ever shown to you.
- **People you share with:** you can share a game you created with one person or with a household. The members of your households see the games in your collection.
- **People with the Owner, Admin or Moderator role** can see what you store, whatever its reach, so they can keep the server in order. They can't see the dietary or accessibility needs on your profile, the details of your copies (what you paid for one, what it is worth, where and when you got it, and your notes about it), including copies you no longer have, or how your ratings changed over time. Only you can. They can see that a game left your collection, and when and why.
- **The operator** can reach everything in the server's database and storage.

## Keeping, exporting and deleting your data

- The server keeps your data until it is removed. Some things you remove are kept but hidden, so they can be restored or their history kept.
- You can't delete your account yourself yet. To delete it, ask the operator. Before an account can be deleted, things you share with other people, such as households and events, may have to be handed over or removed.
- When your account is deleted, the data that is only yours goes with it. The audit log keeps its record of past changes.
- The app can't give you a copy of your data yet. To get one, ask the operator.

## Changes to this policy

This policy comes with the date it last changed. When the policy changes, the date changes too, so you can tell whether it has changed since you last read it.

The people who run this server can replace this policy with their own, in any language.
